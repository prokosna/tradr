### The EID's KDF is specified two ways and both are live, found 2026-09-05 by the reading that opens M7

**M6 opened by reading docs/03 against `tradr-discovery` and found `linked_accounts` had been on the wire since M0. M7 opened the same way and found a contradiction instead.** [docs/05](docs/05-security.md#algorithms)'s Algorithms table said the KDF is `HKDF-SHA256` and named EID derivation as its use; [docs/11](docs/11-account-linking.md#deriving-the-link-secret) listed "the EIDs" among the values that are BLAKE3. Both documents are current and nothing said which was right. DCR-082 and [ADR-0018](docs/adr/0018-blake3-derive-key-for-eids.md) settle it as `BLAKE3::derive_key`, and the full account is in [RECORD.md](RECORD.md).

**What makes it worth a section is that the false half was load-bearing three days ago.** DCR-066 chose BLAKE3 for the Link Secret on 2026-09-02 partly *because* the EIDs were already BLAKE3 -- so its stated reason appealed to a list containing one item its own document set contradicted. **The conclusion survives and the reasoning does not**, and that is the entry: it was an argument from a premise nobody had checked, in a file whose recurring lesson is to probe for the artifact instead of for a document's opinion of it.

**The route the decision took out of the live set is the part that generalises.** [ADR-0006](docs/adr/0006-blake3-for-content-integrity.md)'s closing line named the EID a deliberate HKDF exception, and that ADR is **Superseded -- by [ADR-0016](docs/adr/0016-bao-verified-streaming-costs-6-percent.md), which is about `bao`'s per-chunk overhead and says nothing about a KDF at all.** So a decision was carried out of the document set as collateral of a supersession that was not about it, and only docs/05's one-row restatement kept it readable. **Nothing in this repository can notice that**: `ci/state-sync.sh` checks that a DCR reaches `docs/`, and no check reads a superseded ADR for claims its supersession did not replace.

**Two further things were undecided and neither is cosmetic.** `EID = HKDF-Expand(...)` names Expand, which takes a pseudorandom key, and the table beneath it feeds Expand a bootstrap secret derived from `account_id` -- a structured, low-entropy, **public** string, which is precisely what HKDF-Extract exists to condition. And `bootstrap_secret = HKDF(account_id, "tradr-bootstrap-v1")` "named no hash, no salt and no output length", which are DCR-066's own words about the Link Secret line, applying unchanged to a second line one milestone later. **The same omission twice is a pattern**, and the exit taken was to settle every open sub-decision in the ADR rather than the primitive alone: where the window sits, its width and byte order, and `div_euclid` against `/`.

### The advertisement's UUID cannot be allocated and its 31 bytes were never added up, found 2026-09-05 before cutting `WI-M7-002`

**`WI-M7-002`'s Definition of Done says the 31-byte budget is enforced rather than assumed, and enforcing a budget means knowing what is inside it.** Adding it up found three undecided things in fourteen lines, and DCR-082 was the same shape one Work Item earlier. [ADR-0019](docs/adr/0019-a-128-bit-service-uuid-for-the-ble-advertisement.md) and DCR-083 settle it; the full account is in [RECORD.md](RECORD.md).

**`Service UUID, 16-bit, one allocated value` describes something nothing can satisfy.** A 16-bit UUID is an entry in the Bluetooth SIG's registry, assigned to a member; Tradr is not one, and writing the line down does not obtain a value. **That is DCR-082's sentence again** -- a line reading as settled, in a document that has shipped, naming a thing that does not exist -- and it is the second instance in two Work Items, both found by the same act of adding up what a document claims before instructing anyone to build it.

**The block counted a field list and not an encoding, and the difference is whether it fits.** An AD structure carries a length and a type byte before its data, and a discoverable advertisement carries a Flags structure as well; the block's own numbers sum to 14, and what those 14 cost on the air is between 19 and 33 depending on the container. **33 does not fit in 31**, so the budget the block claimed to fit inside had never been compared with the encoding it described. With the 128-bit UUID now decided, Tradr's own budget is 28 bytes and the encoding is exactly 28 -- **no slack, which is why the two reserved bytes are gone.**

**The third is the one an Implementer would have decided silently.** One flags byte was to carry "platform and capability flags", and `Capabilities` is a `u16` with bits 7-15 reserved for Change Drill D10 while `Platform` is a deliberately open string so D7's iOS cannot make a device invisible. **One byte holds neither, let alone both.** The narrowing decided here -- a 4-bit platform code above `Capabilities` bits 0-3 **in their own positions**, so it is a mask and not a mapping -- is a decision about what a scanner is entitled to know before it has authenticated anything, which is not an encoder's decision to take.

### The pin the whole of Tier 0 linking rests on is decided by a line no test reaches, found 2026-09-03

**`PeerExpectation::Device(inviter_device_id)` changed to `PeerExpectation::Unpinned` leaves `cargo test --workspace` entirely green** -- measured here, not asserted. That line is [docs/11](docs/11-account-linking.md#how-bobs-reply-reaches-alice-and-what-authorises-the-connection)'s "The QR is the pin" and nothing else in this repository says it. **This is the third Work Item running with the same sentence in it**, after `WI-M6-006c`'s five `linked_accounts` call sites and `WI-M6-006d`'s retained rung, and the cause is identical every time: the line lives inside a `#[tauri::command]` whose eight `State<'_, T>` extractors nothing in `crates/` can construct. It is DF-36.

**What makes it a Deferred entry rather than a `REVISE` was measured too, and it is worth more than the entry.** Removing the pin does not link a stranger, because a second instrument catches it: the key join inside `verify_link` compares `BLAKE3(invite.identity_pub)[0..16]` against the `DeviceId` the channel authenticated, so a device answering at the inviter's address that does not hold her key fails verification and no Link is stored. **`verification_is_asked_about_the_channels_device_id_never_one_recomputed_from_the_invite` is that measurement**, and it is the test the review's own first mutation confirmed -- it fails, alone, the moment `authenticated_peer` stops being `channel.peer()`. So the pin is the cheap check and the key join is the load-bearing one, which is the order docs/11 puts them in.

**Six mutations were run here rather than the diff read, and four failed exactly the test naming them.** A recomputed `authenticated_peer` failed test 4 alone; a `max_frame_size` that is not the channel's failed the two round-trip tests, which is [docs/04](docs/04-protocol.md#a-control-stream-may-open-with-linkreply-instead-of-hello)'s "both bounds stay the channel's own" made observable; a `dial_target` deriving the id from the agreement key instead of the identity key failed test 5 alone. **The two that stayed green are the finding**: the pin above, and the clock-skew allowance dropped to zero.

**The skew constant is covered in one direction and it is the direction that matters, which is the opposite of `WI-M6-005b`'s cap.** `invite_skew_secs` raised to 100,000,000 fails the expiry test; dropped to `0` fails nothing. docs/11 says a too-generous allowance costs nothing here, because the most it can do is let the replier answer an invite the inviter will refuse -- so **the uncovered direction is the harmless one and the covered direction is the dangerous one**. That is worth writing down precisely because `WI-M6-005b` recorded the same shape with the polarity reversed, where `INVITE_BLOB_MAX_CHARS` failed a test only when it shrank and never when it grew.
